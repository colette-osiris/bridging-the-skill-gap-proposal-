
# Bridging the Skill Gap: A Bioinformatics Workshop Proposal

A proposal developed for the Community Engagement Committee of the Department of Cell Biology and Molecular Genetics (CBMG) at the University of Maryland College Park, outlining an optional, extra-curricular workshop/seminar series to bridge the skill gap between introductory bioinformatics exposure and professional application in undergraduate life science education.

**Author:** Colette Rouiller

**Status:** Proposal stage — drafted and finalized; intended for submission to the CBMG Community Engagement Committee.

## Why This Proposal Exists

Undergraduate biology education traditionally emphasizes theoretical knowledge and wet-lab techniques, but advances in high-throughput sequencing, large-scale data collection, and computational modeling have re-categorized computational technique education from a niche skill to a critical one. Educational institutions have struggled to adapt at the same pace: a 2018 NCBI study reported that 95% of surveyed faculty agreed bioinformatics education should be integrated into undergraduate life sciences, but only 32% reported teaching courses that cover any bioinformatics-related content. The Society of Experimental Biology has reported that approximately 76% of trainees are self-taught in bioinformatics skills, while only about 20% acquire these skills through formal coursework.

This proposal addresses the gap not at the introductory level (where many workshops already exist) but specifically between introductory concepts and professional application: students who have completed introductory programming and have biological mechanistic background but who lack hands-on training in commonly-used professional tools, workflows, and reproducibility practices.

## Proposal Structure

The proposal is organized around a four-phase implementation plan:

1. **Program Objectives and Target Outcomes** — Primary objectives, expected outcomes (each participant produces a portfolio-ready GitHub repository), and target participant profile.
2. **Series Structure and Curriculum Design** — 7-week sequence covering -omics data and file formats, sequencing alignment/mapping, data analysis and visualization, HPC and SSH for remote execution, workflow systems (Nextflow/Snakemake), Git/GitHub for version control, and an end-to-end pipeline.
3. **Implementation Plan** — Logistics, instructional team structure, funding model (open-source tools, UTA-program-compatible TA support), technical environment requirements, and recruitment.
4. **Support and Evaluation Measures** — Office hours, communication infrastructure, evaluation metrics (pre/post self-assessments, repository reviews, feedback surveys), and future development pathways.

The proposal emphasizes a low-cost, low-barrier, scalable approach designed to bypass the administrative constraints that typically apply to limited-enrollment programs in bioinformatics-adjacent departments (information science, biology, computer science).

## About the Infographic

"How DNA Becomes Data" is one of multiple supplemental materials envisioned for the workshop series. It illustrates the six-step process by which a biological sample is collected, extracted, library-prepped, sequenced, digitally converted, and stored as a sequence file (FASTQ/FASTA). It was designed as a learning aid for the Week 1 module on -omics data and file formats.
